# E0009 Red-Team Checklist

E0009 was motivated by a known failure and is therefore at high risk of hindsight overfitting.

A red-team review should test at least these objections:

1. **Retrospective flexibility** — would the proposed mixture have retained many extra coordinates, making any apparent rescue trivial?
2. **Matched exposure** — compare all rescue variants at exactly the same total K / probability exposure.
3. **Structural duplication** — determine whether HLR, VVD-R, slot density and S1 routing are mostly restatements of exact order-statistic geometry.
4. **Convergence double counting** — correlated recurrence, shadow and exact-state features cannot be counted as independent votes without residualization.
5. **Tail-rescue leakage** — rescue candidates must be chosen by a predeclared rule before the target and must not be manually chosen from visually unusual coordinates.
6. **S1 causal language** — S1 is a sorted-state routing anchor only; no physical causal claim is permitted from sorted data.
7. **PowerBall tiny samples** — exact-state successor and VVD-successor distributions require strong shrinkage toward the 1/16 structural field.
8. **K13 interaction** — verify that any improvement arises before K13 compression rather than from silently increasing candidate exposure.
9. **Multiple testing** — record all rescue fractions, weighting schemes and confidence thresholds inspected.
10. **Negative evidence** — if the distribution-first model does not improve proper scores or candidate recall prospectively, simplify or reject it rather than preserving it narratively.

Strongest falsification target: E0009 fails if, at matched exposure, it does not reduce catastrophic exclusions or improve paired probability/candidate-recall metrics versus the simpler incumbent and exact structural controls.
