# Grounding Q&A — Coalition Assembly Research

**Date:** 2026-08-01  
**Status:** unresolved questions recorded for prospective testing

## Q1. Did the repository executable generator itself place all five 2026-07-31 winners in one frozen candidate pool?

**No evidence yet.** The 5/5 recall belongs to the conversationally published candidate hierarchy from the 2026-07-31 forecast. The repository's currently inspected research scaffold does not reproduce that exact manual hierarchy. Future work should encode the same expert-preserving candidate logic so that pre-draw candidate recall is machine-reproducible.

## Q2. Does the new maximum-coverage selector predict the exact winning five-number line?

**No.** It improves the conditional probability of retaining at least three winning candidates together across a ten-line portfolio. Under a uniform posterior over all five-subsets of the candidate pool, exact 5/5 probability remains `10/C(n,5)` for any ten distinct lines.

## Q3. What information is required to improve exact 5/5 assembly?

A validated non-uniform coalition posterior. Candidate node evidence alone is insufficient unless pair or higher-order interactions carry real prospective information. Candidate features, pair affinities and hyperedge factors must be evaluated target-blind and against matched random/null controls.

## Q4. Did dual-adjacent-pair logic solve the 2026-07-31 line?

**Not prospectively.** Retrospective pair-of-pairs + anchor generation ranked the true line 19th among 417 generated challenger lines, and produced nearby 4-main lines. This is useful diagnostic evidence but cannot be called a pre-draw success because the assembler was developed after the result was known.

## Q5. Should HEPS shrink the candidate pool from 17/18 to 14?

**Not yet.** A 14-number pool dramatically improves combinatorial coverage if all five winners survive compression, but the tested role-aware compression degraded recall on the legacy holdout benchmark. Compression requires its own prospective recall gate.

## Q6. Why use the user-supplied pre-2026 workbook at all?

Only as a legacy structural stress-test and algorithm-development dataset. It was not used to modify the active mechanical-era ledger, is not committed, and must not become an active prediction dependency. The repository doctrine remains authoritative.

## Q7. What was the strongest negative result?

Most score-ranked assemblers underperformed the matched-random conditional 3+ baseline in the 98-target oracle-conditioned benchmark. This indicates that sophisticated scoring can increase confidence without increasing information. The portfolio optimizer should therefore retain a strong uniform/coverage component until predictive coalition weights prove themselves prospectively.

## Q8. What should be frozen for the next unseen targets?

At minimum:

1. candidate pool and per-expert scores;
2. current HEPS ten-line portfolio;
3. unweighted maximum-coverage ten-line portfolio;
4. posterior-weighted coverage challenger;
5. pair/hypergraph challenger;
6. matched random control with identical pool size and line count.

All artifacts must be created before the target draw and scored without regeneration afterward.

## Q9. Does this research change Matrix B / PowerBall logic?

No. This research addresses Matrix A coalition assembly only. The separate PowerBall model and its 2026-07-31 top-ranked PB11 hit should be evaluated under its own prospective protocol.

## Q10. What evidence would justify a core merge?

A sequence of unseen mechanical-era targets showing that the challenger improves primary HEPS KPIs relative to both current HEPS and matched random, without relying on retrospective rule changes. A single 3+, 4+ or exact hit is insufficient on its own; cumulative submitted-line exposure and null probability must be reported.
